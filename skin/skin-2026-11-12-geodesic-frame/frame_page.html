Sizing

%
The offset is the gap between the polyp hole and the circle through the pentagon's corners. The pentagon's side is also the spoke spacing. The tip diameter cannot be smaller than the width. Where a spoke runs into a pentagon the radius is the transition radius but never more than a third of the hole diameter. Where two spokes meet at a node and their straight edges would run into each other inside the tip circle, that corner is rounded until it is tangent to the tip circle.
Reading the patch. Grey = the parts of this patch: pentagon plates with their arms, joined into one flat piece. Each spoke keeps the direction it has seen from straight above the plate it hinges on, so nothing is stretched; the curve of the skin comes from folding. Dashed lines are bend lines: on a spoke leaving a pentagon, perpendicular to the spoke and tangent to the circle inscribed in the pentagon; on a free tip, across the spoke where the tip circle meets its edges. The number beside a pentagon spoke is how far it bends. Orange numbers and purple S mark where loose spokes and spine spokes rivet. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, double-click to reset.
